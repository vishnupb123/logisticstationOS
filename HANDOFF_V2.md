**What this is.** This document is the build brief for **StationOS v2**. It contains:
- everything learned from a full, ordered study of the owner's v2 reference material (38 screenshots of real station tooling plus a definitions sheet);
- an honest audit of v1 against that reality;
- the v2 product thesis and the pitch to a site manager;
- the architecture changes, twelve specified epics with acceptance criteria, a phased plan, a quality bar, risks and open questions.

It is written so that a new engineering session (human or AI) can build v2 without this conversation.

**How it relates to the other docs.**
- `HANDOFF.md` remains the record of v1 (milestones 1–10 and the live-station work of 2026-11-08 → 11-12).
- This file is the plan for what comes next.
- Where they disagree about *what to build*, this file wins; for *how v1 works today*, `HANDOFF.md` and the ADRs win.

Written: 2026-11-13. Source material: `V2/Images/` (38 screenshots) and `V2/Information/operations_acronyms_and_metric_definitions.txt`. Both are **gitignored and must never be committed** (see §0).

## 0. Ground rules that carry into v2

These are non-negotiable and come from `CLAUDE.md`. v2 changes none of them.

1. **No real operational data in the repository.** That covers screenshots, employee names or logins, station or node codes, trip/plan/container IDs, real volumes and proprietary tool names or branding.
   - `images/` and `V2/` are gitignored. **Learn concepts only.** This document describes processes generically on purpose: it names no tools, sites or people, and uses no figures copied from the screens.
   - Where a screen showed a number, this document gives at most a *shape* (e.g. "front-loaded", "peaks late morning").
2. **Synthetic data only**, generated by seeded generators. Any future real-data adapter needs a new ADR and the owner's explicit approval first (§14, open question Q1).
3. **Metric provenance is explicit.** Every metric and parameter carries one of three provenance tags:
   - `documented`: the owner supplied the definition in the V2 definitions sheet;
   - `observed`: a label or column seen on a screen, with semantics inferred;
   - `proposed`: our assumption.
   
   Never present `observed` or `proposed` as `documented`.
4. **Decision support only (ADR-0009).** Autopilot acts on the in-memory twin. Anything that would act on the real world goes through a `DecisionRecord` approval.
5. **Other carried rules:**
   - `FakeLLMProvider` is test-only.
   - RBAC is a *claimed* role (not real auth) and is labelled as such in the UI.
   - No wildcard CORS.
   - No endpoint accepts filesystem paths.
   - No scraping of real ferry or transport sites.
   - Commit or push only when asked.
   - Commits end with `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`.

## 1. Executive summary

### What v1 is

v1 is a working local-first station "brain" with five parts.

**1. A package-level digital twin of a delivery station's whole day** (SimPy, deterministic per seed):
- linehauls with ferry delays;
- induct, sort, stow, pick, stage;
- the high-value cage;
- yard and VSA;
- DSP drivers on a generated road map;
- delivery attempts and RTS;
- after-dispatch close-out (GTDR, ferry checks, truckfile revisions, scrubs, VET/VTO, tomorrow's plan);
- chaining into the next day from the final truckfile.

**2. Six specialist agents plus an orchestrator** (inbound, sort/stow, outbound, dispatch, quality, shift planning). They:
- detect risks;
- simulate counterfactual plans over belief futures;
- act in observe, advise or autopilot mode;
- learn a situation-conditioned playbook.

**3. A live runtime and about 20 live pages**, with polling, a session bar and notifications.

**4. A Station Assistant.** It combines:
- deterministic root-cause analysis;
- BM25 retrieval with cited sources;
- a number-checked local-LLM summary;
- agent and task coordination.

**5. Governance.** Approvals, RBAC (claimed role), audit, and an observed-outcome replay.

### What the v2 study showed

The owner's v2 material shows the *real* station's tooling and definitions. Against it, v1 is **directionally right but not yet credible to a site manager** for four reasons:

1. **Five headline metrics are computed differently from how the station defines them.** These are Stow WIP, Est. Stow WIP Compliance, Induct FPH, Lost Hours and Induct Downtime DPMO. A site manager will notice within a minute. This is the single biggest pitch risk (§5.1).
2. **The station runs on concepts v1 doesn't model:**
   - cycles as first-class objects (including a separate high-value cycle and "unplanned" volume);
   - an automated sort line alongside manual induct;
   - containers (gaylords/pallets) per linehaul;
   - a full package status tree;
   - problem-solve state machines;
   - hourly headcount by process path;
   - job rotation by trained and disallowed paths;
   - VET/VTO as *opportunities with sign-up windows* (associates submit, the station accepts).
3. **The day's timing and scale differ from our presets.** Trucks land the evening before; pick starts later; real volume is materially larger than the 12k `normal_day`.
4. **The station's working surface is "work items" with expiry and ownership.** v1 spreads the same idea across notifications, close-out tasks and agent recommendations.

### What v2 is

> **StationOS v2 = the station's own language and numbers, a planner that thinks a day ahead, and agents that turn every exception into an owned, expiring work item, with an assistant that can explain any number on any screen.**

v2 is **fidelity first**: match the definitions, timeline, objects and screens a manager already knows. Then it is **leverage**: forecasting, planning, rotation and agent automation on top of that fidelity. The pitch only works if the first part is right.

## 2. The station day, in the correct order

This was reconstructed from the screenshots (taken across one evening, with the next-day plan and the current day's state visible) and the owner's earlier explanations (`HANDOFF.md` §1a). Times are **shape, not data**: they are approximate, observed on one sampled day, and must be **configurable per site** in v2 (Epic E2).

| # | When (approx.) | What happens | Who | v1 coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **D-1 afternoon → night** | Road linehauls arrive and are parked **"In Yard"** at a door with an **unload sequence**. Further trips are "Pending Depart" or "In-Transit" overnight, some on multi-leg routes with a ferry crossing. **Manifested volume keeps growing**; much of it is "unplanned" (not in the container plan) until it arrives. | Yard marshal, inbound | ◐ trips + ferry ETAs; no yard-parked trailers, unload sequence or planned/unplanned split |
| 2 | **D-1 evening** | The **next-day shift plan** is run (and can be re-run). It covers: <br>• volume per cycle (total / planned / sideline-RTS / planned rollover); <br>• total show hours vs. needed hours; <br>• planned TPH (with and without indirect "5s" time); <br>• work groups with time windows, attendance %, expected headcount and show hours; <br>• an **hourly headcount grid by process path**. | Manager | ◐ v1 close-out builds a plan; no hourly path grid or work groups |
| 3 | **D-1 evening** | **VET/VTO opportunities** are posted per time frame, each with a **sign-up window**. Associates **submit**; the station **accepts** up to need. A recommendation table shows how many more opportunities to post "assuming no more acceptance of submitted ones". | Manager, associates | ◐ v1 models offers that associates accept with a probability; the semantics are inverted (§5.5) |
| 4 | **Pre-shift** | **Shift rotation** is run for the delivery date: <br>• each associate has a rotation priority (None/Low/High), *disallowed* paths (recent heavy paths), trained paths and a **recommended path**; <br>• a pre-shift plan tab re-plans for the day; <br>• handheld devices are verified. | Manager, PA | ◐ v1 has a ≤2-consecutive-days rule only |
| 5 | **~01:30** | Early crews start: the RTS work group and an early single-cycle crew. | RTS, sort | ✗ v1 has no RTS crew or work-group windows |
| 6 | **~02:30** | The main sort crew starts. **Receive** works by unload sequence. **Induct** splits into an **automated line**: <br>• an auto-scan induct loader; <br>• a labeler that applies the sort label; <br>• pushers and diverters; <br>• an auto-divert onto **fingers / container building**; <br>• a stow buffer per finger. <br><br>It also has **manual lines**, plus non-conveyable manual handling. Then: **stow** to clusters/aisles (standard clusters plus special zones), **problem solve**, the jackpot, sidelining. | Sort | ◐ manual induct lines + stow; no automated line, fingers or containers |
| 7 | **Throughout sort** | The station watches: <br>• **Induct FPH** (rolling 10 min) vs. the **target derived from remaining volume ÷ remaining belt run time**; <br>• **Stow FPH**; <br>• **Stow WIP in minutes** with lower/upper thresholds and **15-min-interval compliance**; <br>• **lost hours** from belt gaps; <br>• **downtime DPMO**; <br>• quality (no-read, multi-read, misapplied label) with the **top impacted line**; <br>• induct dwell > 60 min per cluster; <br>• pending re-induct. | Sort lead, AM | ◐ v1 has most metrics but **different formulas** (§5.1) |
| 8 | **~10:00** | **Pick starts for the cycle**, and induct for that cycle is **blocked**. The tool shows a global banner telling the user to change the cycle. Late volume must be moved: <br>• **change cycle**; <br>• **force induct**; <br>• **enable a cycle for N hours**; <br>• next day. <br><br>Sort tails off within roughly an hour. | AM, sort lead | ◐ v1 has the pick-start block and rollover; no cycle management actions |
| 9 | **~10:00 → ~13:30** | **Pick & stage**: <br>• picklists per associate (with idle time between picklists); <br>• staging locations by lane; <br>• pick-stage problem solve; <br>• yard assist & cart handling. <br><br>Meanwhile: **OTR support** from mid-morning; **next-day pick** crew in parallel; **yard marshals** all day. | Outbound | ◐ v1 picks/stages/dispatches but **~1.5 h earlier** than observed |
| 10 | **Late morning → afternoon** | Dispatch → on road → delivery failed / rejected / drop failed → **RTS inbound**. Returns flows cover customer returns, undeliverables and partner shipments. | DSP, RTS | ✅ v1 dispatch, delivery attempts, RTS (simplified statuses) |
| 11 | **After dispatch** | Close-out, all already modelled in v1 (`closeout.py`): <br>• GTDR (Global Trailer Dock and Release) for late and departing trailers; <br>• ferry checks and truckfile revisions (never truly final); <br>• **in-station and on-road scrubs** (startable or schedulable); <br>• **review missing / lost packages**; <br>• headcount, VET/VTO and tomorrow's plan. | Supervisor, manager | ✅ v1 close-out; scrubs exist; missing/lost review partial |
| 12 | **All day** | **Work items** (by category, with priority/severity, owner, starter and expiry) are the shared to-do surface: <br>• categories: Induct, Sort, Pick & Stage, Dispatch, Fleet, Safety, Ops Updates, People; <br>• tiles: Pending, High Priority, Completed Today, Expired Today; <br>• pending tasks carry over from previous days. | Everyone | ◐ v1 has a Work Items page, but notifications, close-out tasks and recommendations are three separate things |

**Key structural insight.** The station day is **two overlapping production lines:**
- **Inbound/sort** runs from the night before until pick start.
- **Outbound** runs from pick start until the last van leaves.

**Planning for tomorrow runs in parallel with today**: the next-day pick crew, the next-day plan and VET/VTO windows. v1 treats tomorrow as something that happens after dispatch. v2 should make "tomorrow" a live object from the evening before today ends (E6/E7).

## 3. Glossary and metric definitions (with provenance)

### 3.1 Metrics the owner has defined (`documented`)

These come from the owner's definitions sheet. They are rewritten generically, without tool names. **v2 must implement these exact formulas** and label them `documented`.

| Metric | Definition (documented) | v1 today | v2 change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stow WIP** (minutes) | `(inducted packages pending stow ÷ Stow FPH) × 60`. Lower and upper thresholds **default to 15 and 30 min** and can be adjusted in Settings. | Queued-work minutes, **proposed band 0.5–10 min** | Replace the formula. Thresholds come from Settings (default 15/30); a site may tighten them. |
| **Est. Stow WIP Compliance** | `intervals within [lower, upper] ÷ total intervals × 100%`, computed in **15-minute intervals**. Target = the network goal; Actual = real-time. | Share of **5-min** samples | Use 15-min intervals. Show the target as a Settings value (the proposed 70% stays until the owner confirms). |
| **Induct FPH** | Actual = inducted volume in the **past rolling 10 minutes, expressed per hour**. Target = `total volume remaining ÷ remaining belt run time`. | Trailing **60 min**; static target | Rolling 10 min × 6. Add a **dynamic target**. |
| **Belt run time** | `sort end − sort start − (fast start + breaks + lunch)`. | Not modelled | Add a sort start/end per cycle, a fast-start allowance and a **break/lunch schedule** to the site profile. |
| **Stow FPH** | Stowed volume per hour. Target = `volume left to stow ÷ remaining belt run time`. | Stow PPH per stower | Keep per-stower PPH as a secondary measure; add station Stow FPH with its dynamic target. |
| **Total volume** | Volume arrived on site vs. volume inducted. | Partially (funnel) | A dedicated tile. |
| **INC** | Packages with a valid induct scan that are **not stowed**. | Implicit | An explicit count; it feeds Stow WIP. |
| **Lost Hours** | Downtime from **gaps between consecutive packages on one belt** where `20 s < gap < 120 s`; each such gap is a defect. Measured per belt, reported in hours, cumulative. | Line **down time** (outages + jams) | Implement belt-gap accounting in the induct model (needs per-package induct timestamps per line, which v1 already has). Open question Q7: are gaps ≥120 s counted elsewhere as stops? |
| **Induct Downtime DPMO** | `(total downtime stops ÷ total inducted packages) × 1,000,000`. The definition notes inclusions (low-volume operators, a specific induct-code family). | "Packages that could have been inducted during down time ÷ …" | Implement stops/inducted × 1e6. The inclusion rules stay an open question (Q8). |
| **Quality metrics** | No-read, multi-read and misapplied-label rates **per induct line**, as % of packages processed; the "top impact by quality issue" names the worst line. | Station-level rates | Make them per line, and add a "top impacted line". |
| **Missed induct** | Packages that missed the induct scan. | ✗ | Add it as a defect path. |
| **Misapplied label (non-automated)** | Label misapplied, leading to a misdivert. | Rate exists | Link it causally to misdivert. |
| **Misdivert (non-auto-divert)** | Package on the **wrong rack** without an induct defect. It results in a **first sort attempt failure (FSAF)** or a "stow scan to problem solve" defect. | Missort (caught/uncaught) | Rename and split: misdivert → FSAF or stow-to-PS. Add FSAF to associate metrics. |
| **Sortation flow** | Induct vs. stow (flow balance). | Funnel | A chart on Sort Overview. |
| **Induct dwell packages** | Packages **neither inducted nor stowed for > 60 min**, per cluster, including those diverted to the jackpot. | ✗ | A per-cluster tile plus an agent risk. |
| **Tasks for PSA** | Device tasks pending for the problem-solve associate. | PS queue length | Rename; make them work items. |
| **PS — damaged flow** | Pending PS → Pending Approval (manager review) → Pending Stow (repacked, not yet stowed/staged; includes reviewed-but-unprocessed) → Resolved (inducted/stowed) **or** returned to FC / disposed. | Damaged: recoverable vs. not | A state machine with a **manager approval step**, which can be a work item. |
| **PS — misdivert flow** | Misdivert (stower marks "wrong aisle cluster", plus auto-divert errors) → Pending Re-induct ("marked for reprocess") → Resolved (inducted/stowed/staged/in transit). DPMO = misdiverts ÷ packages not scanned by PS. | Missort → PS | A state machine plus a misdivert DPMO. |
| **Pending re-induct** | Packages to re-induct today that are **at risk of missing on-time re-induct**: status "marked for reprocess" within **60 min before the current induct cycle ends**; removed **30 min after** it ends. | ✗ | A time-windowed tile plus an agent risk. |
| **Network missorts** | Packages sidelined at induct as **wrong station** or **wrong carrier**. Pending PS (not re-planned) → Pending Stow (re-planned, not yet in the sort-centre-bound container) → Resolved (stowed/departed). | ✗ | A new upstream-defect generator plus a state machine. |

## 5. v1 audit: where we match reality and where we don't

The items are ordered by pitch risk: how fast a site manager would lose trust.

### 5.1 Metric definitions (critical, fix first)

Five headline metrics disagree with the documented definitions (§3.1): **Stow WIP, Est. Stow WIP Compliance, Induct FPH, Lost Hours, Induct Downtime DPMO**.

- The Stow WIP band is the worst case: v1 flags healthy at 0.5–10 min, while the station's default band is 15–30 min. With v1's computation, a healthy real station would look permanently "over".
- **The agents' detectors and the playbook use these metrics**, so the fix touches `snapshot.py`, `detectors.py` (WIP band risk), `explain.py` and the Assistant's facts.
- **Calibration consequence:** after the switch, re-tune the stow labor plan so that a normal day sits inside 15–30 min most of the time. Otherwise every demo opens with a red tile.

### 5.2 Timeline and scale

| Item | v1 | Observed shape | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linehaul arrivals | Mostly during the shift | Evening before + overnight; trailers wait **in yard** | E3: yard-parked trailers, unload sequence |
| Sort start | 02:00 | ~02:30 main crew, ~01:30 early crews | E2: site profile |
| Pick start | 08:30 | ~10:00 | E2 |
| Waves / departures | 09:20–11:00 | Pick & stage to ~13:30 | E2 |
| Volume | 12k (normal), 18k (peak) | Materially larger per cycle | E2: a `scale` parameter; presets at owner-approved non-identifying ranges (Q2) |
| Hourly receive profile | Flat-ish | Front-loaded with a mid-sort spike | E2: hourly arrival profile |
| Next-day pick | ✗ | A parallel crew late morning | E6 |

### 5.3 Missing physical model: the automated sort line

v1 only has manual induct lines that stow into aisles. Real induct is split **automation vs. manual**:
- The automated line: auto-scan loader → labeler → pusher/diverter → **auto-divert to fingers** that build containers → stow buffer → stow.
- Its defects (no-read, full bay, jackpot rate) are **per finger**.
- Misdiverts are either auto-divert errors or wrong-rack (non-auto-divert) errors.

→ **E5.**

### 5.4 Missing objects

| Object | v1 | Needed for |
|---|---|---|
| **Cycle** (standard, high value, unplanned) as data, with enable/force/active-until | Implicit `CycleConfig` | The pick-start banner, late-volume decisions, dwell/receive splits |
| **Container** (gaylord/pallet) per linehaul | ✗ | Induct sequencing, receive, search |
| **Trip legs** (facility sequence), issues, watch list | Single trip + ferry | Credible inbound tracking |
| **Canonical package status tree** (§4 S6–S8) | ~15 flat statuses | Dwell, ageing, RTS, every drill-down |
| **PS state machines** (damaged, misdivert, network missort) | Queue + outcome | PS pages, the approval step, DPMO |
| **Work group** with shift windows | Roles only | Labor plan, VET/VTO time frames |
| **Process path** catalogue | ~8 roles | Rotation, hourly HC grid, labour hours |
| **Opportunity** (VET/VTO with sign-up window) | Offer → accept | §5.5 |
| **Work item** with expiry/owner/category | 3 separate concepts | §5.6 |
| **Settings** (thresholds, breaks, cycles, delivery windows) | Constants in config | Documented thresholds are "adjustable in Settings" |

### 5.5 VET/VTO semantics are inverted

- **v1:** the station *offers* to specific associates; each accepts with a probability (VET 50% × lead factor, VTO 65%).
- **Real:**
  1. The station posts **opportunities per time frame** with a **sign-up window**.
  2. Associates **submit**.
  3. The station **accepts** up to need. Submitted can exceed accepted, and in some frames far exceeds it.
  4. Recommendations are expressed as "post N more for this time frame".

The fix is a model change, not just a relabel → **E7**.

### 5.6 Exceptions are fragmented

v1 has three parallel surfaces:
- notifications (bell + ack);
- close-out tasks;
- agent recommendations (approve/dismiss).

The station has **one**: the work item. v2 unifies them (ADR-0015 proposed) → **E10**.

### 5.7 Assistant gaps (v1 is strong but)

- **Model:** the 1.5B local model's summaries simplify and sometimes mislabel (numbers are guarded; wording is not).
- **No evaluation set:** answer quality is unmeasured.
- **Topic coverage is missing for:** cycles, containers, quality per line, rotation, plan vs. actual, ageing, missing/lost.

→ **§10.**

### 5.8 What v1 already gets right (keep it)

- Seeded, replayable, package-level twin with interventions as a log.
- Belief futures, so no agent ever sees ground truth.
- Information discipline: ferry revisions are revealed only by a check.
- Next-day chaining from the final truckfile.
- Decisions with measured impact (a replay without the decisions).
- Close-out realism: GTDR, never-final truckfile, scrubs, rotation-aware assignment.
- The yard/VSA, misstage and forgotten-bag floor exceptions, and the sweep / cluster transfer.
- A road-following driver map.
- Grounded assistant answers with cited sources.
- Strong test discipline: 300 backend tests and 38 Playwright tests at the end of v1.

## 6. v2 product thesis and the site-manager pitch

### 6.1 The problem as a site manager lives it

A manager runs the station across **~20 separate screens**: work items, plan, rotation, dwell, ageing, RTS, receive, linehauls, trip tracking, overview, inbound, induct, cycle management, stow, buffers and three associate pages. Each screen is a *mirror*: it shows the present state well. **None of them**:
- tells you **what will happen** at 10:00 if nothing changes;
- compares **what each option would do**;
- **joins** the screens (a late trailer → unplanned volume → stow WIP → pick-start conflict → late routes → DEA);
- **writes tomorrow's plan** from today's reality;
- **explains** last night's DEA drop in plain language with the decisions taken.

That work lives in experienced managers' heads, under time pressure, every day.

### 6.2 The promise (one line)

> **StationOS sees the day before it happens, tests the options, tells you what to do and why, and remembers every decision. You stay in charge.**

### 6.3 Five things it does on day one of a pilot (simulated today)

1. **An early warning with a reason.**
   - *Example:* "Induct is 18% under the target needed to finish by pick start, because line 2's belt gaps cost 41 min. At this rate, about 600 packages roll."
   - The target is the documented *remaining volume ÷ remaining belt run time*.
2. **What-ifs before acting.**
   - *Example:* "Open the spare line now (+0.9 pp DEA), or extend pick start 20 min (+0.6 pp DEA, −2 routes on time)."
   - Each option is simulated on belief futures, so the agent never sees ground truth.
3. **Cycle decisions at pick start.**
   - *Example:* "312 packages are unplanned and still on the floor. Recommend force-inducting 140 (they fit) and moving 172 to the next cycle."
4. **Tomorrow, planned tonight.**
   - It covers:
     - an hourly headcount by path from tomorrow's truckfile;
     - **VET/VTO opportunities to post now, and when the sign-up window should close**;
     - a rotation that respects trained and disallowed paths.
   - It is re-planned automatically when a ferry revision lands.
5. **An assistant that explains anything.**
   - *Example questions:* "Why was DEA low?" "Which line hurt us most?" "How many VTOs were accepted for the late frame?" "What's on GTDR?"
   - Answers come with the numbers, the decisions taken, the measured impact and cited records.

### 6.4 Why trust it

- **The station's own definitions.** Every metric shows its formula and provenance (documented / proposed).
- **Approval-gated.** Nothing touches the real world without a person (ADR-0009). Autopilot exists only inside the simulation.
- **Auditable.** Every recommendation, approval and outcome is logged, and outcomes are re-measured.
- **Honest.** The system says "simulated", "inferred" and "proposed" where those apply. No black-box numbers: the LLM can only restate numbers that the deterministic analysis computed.

### 6.5 How we measure value (pilot KPIs, all `proposed`)

| Value lever | Metric | How measured in shadow mode |
|---|---|---|
| Earlier detection | Lead time between the agent's alert and the manager's own action | Timestamp comparison on the same day |
| Better decisions | ΔDEA / ΔOTD of the recommended vs. the chosen action | Counterfactual replay of both on the twin |
| Planning effort | Manager minutes spent on tomorrow's plan | Self-reported before/after |
| Labor accuracy | Show hours − needed hours, per work group | Plan vs. actual |
| VET/VTO timing | Share of opportunities posted ≥ N h before the frame | Opportunity log |
| Close-out completeness | GTDRs open at end of shift; scrubs done; truckfile checked after final | Close-out log |

### 6.6 What we ask for (the pitch close)

The ask is **not** a deployment. It is a **4-week shadow evaluation**, which needs three things:
- one manager's feedback each week;
- **non-identifying** shape data (volume ranges, shift windows, rates) to calibrate the twin;
- agreement on which 5 metrics define success.

Real data integration is a separate, later decision with IT and security, and needs a new ADR.

## 7. v2 architecture

### 7.1 Principles

1. **One canonical domain model** (cycles, containers, statuses, paths, work groups, opportunities, work items). Twin, metrics, agents, views and the Assistant all read the same objects.
2. **The metric engine is separate from the snapshot.** Each metric is a declared object with `id`, `label`, `formula_text`, `provenance`, `unit`, a `thresholds_from` Settings key, and an implementation tested against hand-computed fixtures.
3. **Settings are data.** Per-site thresholds, schedules, cycles and delivery windows live in a `SiteProfile` that is versioned, editable in the UI, and recorded on every session for replay.
4. **Everything actionable is a work item.** Notifications, close-out tasks, agent recommendations, scrubs, reviews and PS approvals share one lifecycle and SLA model.
5. **The twin stays the only source of truth for simulated reality.** Agents keep seeing belief futures, never ground truth.
6. **Backwards compatible.** v1 presets, endpoints and tests keep passing (or are migrated deliberately, with a note in the ADR).

## 8. The twelve v2 epics (specified)

Each epic lists its goal, scope, model/data, algorithm, UI, agent/Assistant changes and **acceptance criteria (AC)**. The IDs are referenced throughout.

### E1. Metric engine with documented formulas (Phase 0, critical)

- **Goal.** Every headline metric matches §3.1 and shows its provenance.
- **Model.** New `backend/app/simulation/station_ops/metrics.py`:
  - a `MetricSpec(id, label, unit, provenance: documented|observed|proposed, formula_text, settings_keys, compute(twin, now))`;
  - `METRIC_DEFINITIONS` in `snapshot.py` becomes generated from it.
- **Implement:**
  - Stow WIP (min) = INC_pending_stow ÷ StowFPH × 60, guarded for StowFPH = 0 (report "∞ / no stow flow" and flag it);
  - compliance over **15-min** intervals;
  - Induct FPH as rolling 10 min × 6;
  - **dynamic targets** for Induct and Stow FPH from remaining volume ÷ remaining belt run time;
  - **Lost Hours** from per-line inter-package gaps in (20 s, 120 s);
  - **Downtime DPMO** as stops ÷ inducted × 1e6;
  - quality **per line** with a top-impacted line;
  - INC; induct dwell > 60 min per cluster; pending re-induct with its (−60, +30) window.
- **Settings.** `stow_wip_lower_min=15`, `stow_wip_upper_min=30`, `stow_wip_compliance_target` (proposed 0.70), `belt_gap_defect_s=(20,120)`, the break/lunch schedule and the fast-start allowance.
- **Calibration.** Re-tune the stowers / stow rate so a normal day has ≥ 70% WIP compliance under the new band. Re-tune the detectors' WIP risk. Re-baseline the measured-results table.
- **UI.**
  - Every metric tile gets an ⓘ **"How this is calculated"** drawer showing the formula, provenance, settings and the current inputs (e.g. "INC 1,240 ÷ Stow FPH 2,980 × 60 = 25.0 min").
  - It also gets a **top-3 impacted** strip where applicable.
- **AC:**
  - [ ] Unit tests with hand-computed fixtures for every documented metric, including edge cases (zero flow, gap exactly 20 s or 120 s, an interval straddling a threshold).
  - [ ] The normal-day preset over seeds 1–10 has median WIP compliance ≥ 70% and no detector false alarm before 03:00.
  - [ ] The Assistant's DEA/sort answers quote the new values, with the formula in the "how" section.
  - [ ] v1 metrics without documentation remain, tagged `proposed`.

### E2. Site profile, timeline and scale (Phase 0)

- **Goal.** The day looks like the station's day.
- **Model.** `SiteProfile` covers:
  - sort start/end per cycle;
  - early crews;
  - pick start;
  - the wave window;
  - the dispatch window;
  - a scale multiplier;
  - the hourly receive profile;
  - the yard-arrival window (D-1 evening);
  - breaks/lunch;
  - cycles;
  - delivery windows.
  
  Presets derive from a profile plus a scenario.
- **Twin changes.**
  - Linehauls arrive from D-1 afternoon and **park in yard**.
  - Doors are assigned by **unload sequence** (FIFO by planned time, with priority for high-value / earliest delivery window).
  - The `run_until` start moves to D-1 18:00 (configurable). The UI clock shows D-1.
- **Cycle manager (with E4).**
  - Cycles are a list: standard, high value, unplanned.
  - Pick start blocks induct for that cycle and raises the **global cycle banner**.
  - New levers: `change_cycle(packages|container|trip → cycle|next_day)`, `force_induct(cycle, minutes)`, `enable_cycle(cycle, hours)`, `disable_cycle`.
  - The delivery-window configuration determines EAD grouping.
- **AC:**
  - [ ] Normal-day timeline milestones within ±15 min of the profile.
  - [ ] Scale ×2 runs within the §7.4 budget.
  - [ ] The banner appears at pick start, and every listed lever is applicable, logged and replayable.
  - [ ] The next-day chain still works.

### E3. Receive v2: trips with legs, containers, planned vs. unplanned (Phase 1)

- **Model.**
  - `Trip(legs=[Leg(from,to,mode=road|ferry, planned, eta, actual)], operator_type=single|team, tractor, trailer, issues[], watch)`.
  - `Container(id, type=gaylord|pallet|bag, trip, cluster, size_mix, cycle, ead, packages)`.
  - Package `planned: bool` (in the container plan) vs. unplanned. A manifest grows over time (manifested ≤ actual).
  - The predicted dispatch volume is a forecast with error (proposed σ).
- **Twin.**
  - Container-level unload and induct (containers queue per line).
  - Unplanned share per trip (proposed 15–40%, seeded).
  - Trip issues (late leg, ferry cancel, missing seal) as generated events.
- **UI.**
  - Receive: overall per cycle (predicted vs. planned vs. arrived; status ladder planned/unplanned).
  - The linehaul table with **group-by** (size / destination / EAD / cycle / cluster), unload sequence, location (door or "on map").
  - Trip drawer: a legs timeline, a mini map, issues, and a watch toggle.
  - Induct → Containers tab: pending / total per container.
- **Agents.** The Inbound agent gets new risks:
  - `unplanned_surge` (unplanned manifest growth beyond the forecast);
  - `unload_sequence_conflict` (an HV or early-EAD trailer queued behind bulk);
  - `container_stuck` (a container part-inducted for more than N minutes).
- **AC:**
  - [ ] The sum of container packages equals the trip volume.
  - [ ] The planned/unplanned ladder reconciles at every tick (property test).
  - [ ] A group-by on any dimension sums to the total.
  - [ ] A trip with a ferry leg revises its ETA only through the ferry provider or a check (information discipline is kept).

### E4. Canonical package status tree and PS state machines (Phase 1)

- **Model.** `status.py`:
  - One enum tree matching §4 S6–S8. Groups: Pending Receive, Pending Induct, Pending Stow, Pending Depart, On Road, Retained/Held (with reasons), Marked Missing, Marked Lost, plus the reverse-leg RTS tree.
  - Every package keeps `status`, `status_since`, `leg (forward|reverse)` and `cycle`.
- **State machines:**
  - **damaged:** pending PS → pending approval (a **work item** for the manager) → pending stow → resolved | returned/disposed;
  - **misdivert:** marked (by a stower or the auto-divert) → pending re-induct → resolved;
  - **network missort:** sidelined at induct (wrong station/carrier) → pending PS → pending stow (to an outbound transfer container) → resolved.
- **Ageing.** Days-in-status buckets come from `status_since` across chained days (the next-day chain carries ageing).
  - "Review missing" and "review lost" are close-out work items.
  - Scrubs move packages between statuses with the v1 rates.
- **UI.**
  - Dwell (by cycle / by delivery window), Ageing (forward/reverse) and RTS use one generic `StatusTree` component.
  - **Every count is a link** to the package list.
  - Each view shows "data last updated".
- **AC:**
  - [ ] Each package is in exactly one status at all times (an invariant test run every tick in debug).
  - [ ] Every documented PS status in §3.1 is reachable in a seeded test.
  - [ ] Ageing carries across a 3-day chain.

### E5. Automated sort line and fingers (Phase 1)

- **Model.**
  - `AutomatedLine(loader_rate, labeler, pushers, fingers=[Finger(bay_capacity, cluster_set)])`, alongside the manual lines.
  - Packages route to automation vs. manual by size (non-conveyables are manual).
  - Fingers fill bays; a full bay diverts to the jackpot.
  - Per-finger no-read and jackpot rates.
  - Misdivert splits into auto-divert errors vs. wrong-rack errors.
- **Paths.** Container building, auto-scan induct loader, labeler, pusher, diverter, straightener and non-con manual handling become staffable paths (with E6).
- **UI.**
  - Stow & Buffers gets a finger table (status, full bay, jackpot rate, no-read rate, current volume, % of station volume, average PPH) with bay details.
  - Overview splits induct volume and PPH into automation vs. manual.
- **Agents.** The Sort/Stow agent gets:
  - `finger_full_bay` (rebalance clusters to another finger / pull a container builder);
  - `automation_no_read_spike` (with a top-impacted line).
- **AC:**
  - [ ] The automation/manual split sums to total induct.
  - [ ] Turning automation off (an outage disruption) reroutes to manual with throughput loss and a visible Lost Hours increase.

### E6. Labor plan v2: work groups and the hourly path matrix (Phase 2)

- **Model:**
  - `WorkGroup(name, window, scheduled, attendance_rate)` → expected HC and show hours;
  - `PathRate(path, units_per_hour, provenance=proposed)`;
  - the **needed-hours matrix** `needed[path][hour] = volume_through_path[hour] ÷ rate[path]`.
- **Plan output (mirrors S2/S4):**
  - per cycle: total / planned / sideline-RTS / planned rollover;
  - total show hours vs. needed hours;
  - planned TPH with and without the indirect allowance;
  - the work-group table;
  - the hourly grid (headcount | hours | volume view).
- **Next day vs. pre-shift.** The *next-day* plan is computed from tomorrow's truckfile (v1 close-out) and re-run on revisions. The *pre-shift* plan is re-run at shift start with actual call-outs.
- **Next-day pick crew** works in parallel late morning (prepares tomorrow's early picks; proposed scope).
- **AC:**
  - [ ] Matrix row sums equal path volumes.
  - [ ] Show − needed per group feeds the E7 recommendations.
  - [ ] The re-run after a truckfile revision changes only the affected hours.
  - [ ] It runs in < 200 ms.

### E7. VET/VTO opportunities with sign-up windows (Phase 2)

- **Model:**
  - `Opportunity(type=VET|VTO, frame, headcount_needed, window_open, window_close, submitted[], accepted[], status)`.
  - Associates **submit**. Behaviour is proposed: the submission probability is a function of lead time, the frame's attractiveness (early or late) and recent hours.
  - The station **accepts** up to need. The acceptance rule is configurable (first come, rotation-aware, or seniority-blind random; Q9).
  - **Recommendations** assume no more acceptance of submitted opportunities: `post = max(0, gap − accepted)` per frame, shown as in S3.
- **Agent.** The Shift Planning agent decides *when* to open and close windows, and how many to post. It is scored by the next-day staffing gap (keep v1's `evaluate()`).
- **UI.** Active opportunities (type, frame, window state, submitted, accepted), recommendations, and a timeline of submissions.
- **Migration.** v1's `offer_vet/offer_vto` levers become `post_opportunity` and `close_window`. v1 tests are rewritten, not deleted.
- **AC:**
  - [ ] Accepted ≤ submitted ≤ eligible.
  - [ ] Posting earlier yields more submissions (a monotonic test).
  - [ ] A peak-day seed closes the staffing gap to ≤ 1 HC per group with agent timing vs. ≥ 4 without.

### E8. Shift rotation engine (Phase 2)

- **Model.** Per associate:
  - `trained_paths`;
  - `history[path][day]`;
  - `disallowed_paths` (derived: a heavy path on ≥ 2 of the last 3 days, rule `proposed`; Q10);
  - `rotation_priority` (None/Low/High from strain and repetition);
  - `recommended_path`.
- **Algorithm.** A min-cost assignment of associates to path slots (from E6's matrix at shift start). Cost = −skill + repetition penalty + disallowed (∞) + priority bonus for rotating high-priority associates. Use `scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment` if scipy is already in the dependency set; otherwise use greedy with repair. Re-runnable.
- **Outputs.** Rotation compliance %, unfilled slots, and associates without a trained path.
- **UI.** The rotation table (associate, priority, disallowed, trained, recommended), with filters and a "re-run rotation" button. A synthetic name generator is already used in v1; **never real names**.
- **AC:**
  - [ ] No assignment to a disallowed or untrained path.
  - [ ] Compliance ≥ the v1 baseline.
  - [ ] Deterministic per seed.
  - [ ] 200 associates assigned in < 100 ms.

### E9. Plan vs. actual and associate performance (Phase 2)

- **Overview grid.**
  - Rows: receive, induct (automation/manual), stow, station TPH, labour hours by group.
  - Planned vs. actual per interval, with a **15 / 30 / 60 / cumulative** toggle.
  - Cells are coloured only by status token, with a text label (dataviz rules).
- **Associate pages per path:**

## 10. Station Assistant v2

v1's architecture stays (ADR-0013): deterministic analysis is authoritative, BM25 retrieval provides cited sources, and the LLM only summarises numbers that appear in the facts. v2 raises coverage and quality.

### 10.1 New answer topics (each a deterministic analysis in `explain.py`)

| Topic | Example question | The answer must contain |
|---|---|---|
| Cycles | "What happened at pick start?" | Packages still on the floor by status; levers used (force/change cycle) with counts; DEA impact |
| Quality per line | "Which line hurt us?" | Per-line no-read / multi / misapplied with the top line; lost hours per line; DPMO |
| Stow WIP | "Why was WIP out of band at 05:00?" | INC, Stow FPH, the formula with values, the interval compliance timeline, actions taken |
| Receive | "How much unplanned volume came in?" | Planned vs. unplanned by stage; top trips; the forecast error |
| Containers | "Which gaylords are still pending?" | Per-trip container list, pending / total |
| Ageing / missing / lost | "What's ageing over 2 days?" | Buckets by status; scrub results; review outcomes |
| Labor plan | "Are we covered tomorrow 10:00–13:00?" | The group × hour gap; opportunities posted, submitted and accepted; recommendations |
| Rotation | "Who can't do stow tomorrow and why?" | Disallowed reasons (counts, not people, unless a manager role) |
| Work items | "What expired today?" | Expired by category with the source |
| Plan vs. actual | "Were we on plan at 07:00?" | Grid row deltas |

### 10.2 Model and quality

- **Default model upgrade.** It is chosen by an eval, not by taste. Candidates: `qwen2.5:7b` or another local ~7B instruction model, with the 1.5B kept as a low-RAM fallback. This is local only, with no network LLM, unless the owner decides otherwise under ADR-0008.
- **Golden evaluation set** (`backend/tests/runtime/assistant_eval/`):
  - **≥ 60 questions** across the topics, on 3 seeded days, each with expected facts (numbers and entities) and a forbidden list (no invented numbers).
  - Metrics:
    - **fact recall** ≥ 0.9 for the deterministic part;
    - **grounding violations** = 0 for the LLM part;
    - **topic routing accuracy** ≥ 0.95.
  - It runs with `FakeLLMProvider` in CI and the real model locally.
- **Answer contract.** Every answer contains these, in order:
  1. a headline;
  2. "what happened" with numbers;
  3. why (causes, ranked by impact);
  4. what was done (decisions with who, when and the measured impact);
  5. what to do now (a work item link, if any);
  6. sources.
- **Latency.** The deterministic part streams first; the LLM summary arrives after it and never blocks.

## 11. UX and polish standard

The pitch is won or lost here. Rules:

1. **Use the station's vocabulary** (§3) everywhere. Before the pitch, do a vocabulary pass on every page title, column and tooltip.
2. **"Data last updated <sim time>"** under every live table. The **global cycle banner** shows cycle state (sorting / pick started, induct blocked / closed) with the actionable levers.
3. **Every number is explainable:** an ⓘ drawer (formula + inputs + provenance) and a link to the list behind any count.
4. **Interval toggles** (15 / 30 / 60 / cumulative) and **group-by / split** controls behave identically everywhere (one component each).
5. **Status colours are reserved** (good / warning / serious / critical), always paired with an icon and a label. No series colour for status (dataviz skill).
6. **Empty and loading states** say what will appear and when (e.g. "Pick hasn't started; picklists appear at 10:00").
7. **Dark mode, light mode and 390 px mobile** have zero horizontal overflow (keep the v1 e2e check).
8. **Accessibility:** a keyboard path through every action, visible focus, WCAG AA contrast, and labelled charts with a table view.
9. **Presenter mode** (E12): large type, chapter navigation, and hidden dev controls (the role picker stays visible but compact, with its "not real auth" warning).
10. **Copy rules:**
    - Never "live" for simulated data. Badges: *Simulated run*, *Synthetic data*.
    - "Inferred" on LLM text; "Proposed" on assumed metrics.

## 13. Quality bar and verification

1. **Tests first for formulas.** Every `documented` metric has a hand-computed fixture test. Every state machine gets a reachability test, and every reconciliation (planned + unplanned = total; containers = trip; group-bys sum) gets a property test.
2. **Invariants in debug mode.** Each package is in exactly one status. Counts are conserved across statuses. No agent reads truth fields (keep the v1 belief-future guard).
3. **Calibration suite.** For seeds 1–10 × each preset, assert metric ranges (proposed) and timeline milestones. It fails loudly when a change shifts a normal day out of band.
4. **Assistant eval** (§10.2) in CI with `FakeLLMProvider`; the real-model run is a documented manual step.
5. **E2E (Playwright).**
   - Keep all v1 specs.
   - Add: the cycle banner + force induct; a metric drawer; work-item lifecycle; opportunity posting; rotation re-run; container search timeline; the demo chapters.
   - Keep the console-error guard and the 390 px overflow check.
6. **Performance checks** (§7.4) as a pytest marker `perf`, run before each phase exit.
7. **Run it for real** before claiming done. Click through the demo script in light, dark and mobile; screenshot and look at them. This is the v1 discipline: verify, don't assume.
8. **Docs updated with every epic:** ADR, `STATION_PROCESS_MODEL.md` (feature map), `API_REFERENCE.md`, `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`, `HANDOFF.md`, README.

## 14. Governance, privacy and security

- **Reference material.** `images/` and `V2/` are gitignored. Never paste their content (names, logins, codes, IDs, figures) into code, fixtures, docs, tests, commit messages or LLM prompts. Before every commit, run `git status --ignored` and `git diff --cached` and check for leaks.
- **People data in the twin** is synthetic (generated names and logins). Per-associate performance views are role-gated and framed as coaching support, with aggregates by default (E9).
- **Real data (Q1).** Nothing in v2 ingests real exports. If the owner wants a shadow pilot on real data, write ADR-0017 first. It must cover:
  - which data;
  - where it lives (never in the repo);
  - retention;
  - access;
  - redaction;
  - which approvals are needed (site IT/security).
- **Autonomy boundary.** Unchanged: autopilot acts on the in-memory twin only. Real actions mean a `DecisionRecord` plus human approval.
- **Auth.** The claimed role remains a known gap. Phase 3 replaces it, and until then the UI keeps saying so.
- **LLM.** Local by default, and the prompt holds computed facts only (no raw package lists or people data). The grounding check is unchanged.
